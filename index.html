Medications

Furosemide

Class: Loop Diuretic (Sulfonamide derivative)

Chemical Name: 4-Chloro-N-furfuryl-5-sulfamoylanthranilic acid

Molecular Weight: 330.74 g/mol

Chemical Structure: Anthranilic acid derivative with sulfonamide group

Mechanism of Action: Inhibits Na-K-2Cl cotransporter in thick ascending loop of Henle, preventing sodium and chloride reabsorption

Pharmacodynamics: Increases urine output with enhanced excretion of sodium, potassium, calcium, chloride, magnesium, and bicarbonate. Also acts as mild venodilator, decreasing systemic venous pressure

Pharmacokinetics: Bioavailability 40-50% (oral), 86-91% protein bound, elimination half-life ~1 hour (IV), 6 hours (oral)

Purpose: Reduces fluid retention and decreases cardiac workload

Dosage: As prescribed by veterinarian

Timing: Morning meal with food

Onset of Action: 60 minutes (oral), 5 minutes (IV)

Duration: 6 hours (oral), 2-3 hours (IV)

Monitor for signs of dehydration and electrolyte imbalances. Cats are more sensitive to furosemide than dogs.
Pimobendan (Vetmedin)

Class: Inodilator (Benzimidazole-pyridazinone derivative)

Chemical Name: (±)-4,5,6,7-Tetrahydro-6-oxo-3-[4-(2-oxo-1,2-dihydro-pyridin-3-yl)phenyl]-1H-pyridazin-3-one

Molecular Weight: 334.4 g/mol

Chemical Structure: Phenylbenzimidazole skeleton with pyridazinone ring

Mechanism of Action: Dual mechanism - sensitizes cardiac myofilaments to calcium (positive inotropic effect) and inhibits phosphodiesterase III (vasodilatory effect)

Pharmacodynamics: Increases ventricular contractility while reducing preload and afterload without increasing myocardial oxygen consumption

Pharmacokinetics: High oral bioavailability (~70%), extensively protein bound, metabolized by glucuronidation, elimination half-life 2-3 hours

Purpose: Improves heart contractions and reduces blood vessel resistance

Dosage: As prescribed by veterinarian

Timing: Morning meal with food

Onset of Action: Rapid onset within 1 hour

Duration: 6-8 hours

Administer on empty stomach or with small amount of food for best absorption. Not for use in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy.
Apoquel (Oclacitinib)

Class: Janus Kinase (JAK) Inhibitor

Chemical Name: N-Cyclohexyl-4-methyl-3-{[4-methyl-3-(1H-pyrrolo[2,3-d]pyrimidin-4-yl)pyridin-2-yl]amino}benzenesulfonamide

Molecular Weight: 467.56 g/mol

Chemical Structure: Cyclohexylamino pyrrolo[2,3-d]pyrimidine derivative

Mechanism of Action: Selectively inhibits JAK1 enzyme, blocking pro-inflammatory cytokine signaling (IL-2, IL-4, IL-6, IL-13, IL-31)

Pharmacodynamics: Rapidly controls pruritus and inflammation by interrupting the itch-inflammatory cycle

Pharmacokinetics: High bioavailability (89%), rapidly absorbed with peak concentrations in <1 hour, elimination half-life 3-5 hours

Purpose: Controls allergic skin conditions and pruritus

Dosage: As prescribed by veterinarian

Timing: Evening meal with food

Onset of Action: <1 hour

Duration: 12-24 hours

Immunosuppressive effects - monitor for signs of infection. Not recommended for dogs <12 months or those with serious infections.
Trazodone

Class: Serotonin Antagonist and Reuptake Inhibitor (SARI)

Chemical Name: 2-[4-(3-Chlorophenyl)-1-piperazinyl]ethyl]-1,2,4-triazolo[4,3-a]pyridine

Molecular Weight: 325.82 g/mol

Chemical Structure: Triazolopyridine derivative, phenylpiperazine class

Mechanism of Action: Blocks serotonin 2A receptors and inhibits serotonin reuptake, increasing synaptic serotonin levels

Pharmacodynamics: Provides anxiolytic and mild sedative effects by increasing serotonin levels in brain synapses

Pharmacokinetics: Rapid absorption, peak plasma concentration 0.3-1 hour, elimination half-life 3-7 hours, extensively metabolized to active metabolite m-CPP

Purpose: Anxiety management and mild sedation

Dosage: As prescribed by veterinarian

Timing: Evening meal with food

Onset of Action: 30-60 minutes

Duration: 3-12 hours

May enhance effects of other sedatives. Monitor for excessive drowsiness. Effects may take 2-4 weeks for full therapeutic benefit.

Herbal Supplements

Dan Shen (Salvia miltiorrhiza)

Botanical Family: Lamiaceae (Mint family)

Active Compounds: Tanshinones (tanshinone I, tanshinone IIA, cryptotanshinone), salvianolic acids (salvianolic acid A, B), rosmarinic acid, dihydrotanshinone

Molecular Mechanisms: Inhibits NF-κβ signaling pathway, modulates MAPK and Nrf2 pathways, inhibits IL-1, IL-6, and TNF-α expression

Cardiovascular Effects: Coronary vasodilation, anti-platelet aggregation, inhibits low-density lipid oxidation, protects vascular endothelial cells by regulating VCAM-1, ICAM-1, TNF-α

Pharmacokinetics: Poor bioavailability of lipophilic tanshinones, extensive metabolism by CYP450 enzymes

Purpose: Cardiovascular support with blood-thinning properties

Benefits: Coronary vasodilation, anti-platelet aggregation, antioxidant effects

Significant bleeding risk when combined with Hawthorne Berry.
Hawthorne Berry (Crataegus)

Botanical Family: Rosaceae (Rose family)

Active Compounds: Oligomeric procyanidins (procyanidin B2, B5, C1), flavonoids (epicatechin, hyperoside, quercetin, rutin, isoquercitrin), chlorogenic acid, triterpenoids (ursolic acid, corosolic acid, oleanolic acid, maslinic acid)

Molecular Mechanisms: Antioxidant activity via free radical scavenging, inhibition of LDL oxidation, enhancement of cardiac contractility through calcium channel modulation

Cardiovascular Effects: Coronary vasodilation, positive inotropic effects, increases cardiac antithrombin III, decreases liver factor X levels, shows antiplatelet activity

Pharmacokinetics: Contains 20% oligomeric procyanidins (WS® 1442), absorbed in the gastrointestinal tract and distributed to target organs

Purpose: Cardiac support with blood-thinning properties

Benefits: Coronary vasodilation, positive inotropic effects, antioxidant properties

Significant bleeding risk when combined with Dan Shen.
Withannia (Ashwagandha)

Botanical Family: Solanaceae (Nightshade family)

Active Compounds: Withanolides (withaferin A, withanolide A-D, withanone), withanosides (sitoindosides VII-X), alkaloids (withanine, somniferine, tropine), flavonoids

Molecular Mechanisms: Inhibits NF-κβ and STAT pathways, blocks heat shock proteins in cancer cells, modulates GABA receptors, affects HPA axis activity

Therapeutic Effects: Adaptogenic properties, anti-inflammatory (inhibits TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6), neuroprotective (reduces β-amyloid aggregation), immunomodulatory effects

Pharmacokinetics: Contains 0.001-1.5% withanolides by dry weight, bioactive compounds cross blood-brain barrier

Purpose: Stress reduction with indirect cardiovascular benefits

Benefits: Adaptogen properties, anti-inflammatory effects, HPA axis regulation

May interact with Apoquel's immunosuppressive effects.
Passionflower

Botanical Family: Passifloraceae

Active Compounds: Flavonoids (apigenin, quercetin, luteolin, chrysin, vitexin, isovitexin), alkaloids, phenolic acids (GABA, chlorogenic acid), C-glycosyl flavones

Molecular Mechanisms: GABAergic modulation (induces GABA currents in hippocampal slices), binds to benzodiazepine sites of GABAA receptors, inhibits glutamate release and NMDA receptors

Neurological Effects: Anxiolytic through GABA potentiation, neuroprotective through glutamate modulation, modulates opioidergic and nicotinic cholinergic systems

Pharmacokinetics: Flavonoids absorbed in small intestine, crosses blood-brain barrier to exert CNS effects

Purpose: Anxiety relief and mild sedation

Benefits: GABA enhancement, mood improvement, neuroprotection

May enhance sedative effects of Trazodone.
Milk Thistle (Silybum marianum)

Botanical Family: Asteraceae (Daisy family)

Active Compounds: Silymarin (70-80% flavonolignans: silybin A & B, isosilybin A & B, silychristin, silydianin), taxifolin (flavonoid), unsaturated fatty acids (linoleic acid 39.7%, oleic acid 36.7%)

Molecular Mechanisms: Antioxidant through free radical scavenging, inhibits lipid peroxidation, modulates inflammatory pathways by blocking NF-κβ, inhibits CYP3A4 and UGT enzymes

Hepatoprotective Effects: Stabilizes hepatocyte membranes, stimulates protein synthesis, enhances glutathione levels, prevents toxin binding to liver cell receptors

Pharmacokinetics: Poor bioavailability due to limited intestinal absorption, undergoes extensive first-pass metabolism, silybin is the most bioactive component

Purpose: Liver protection and support

Benefits: Hepatoprotective effects, antioxidant properties, potential to modulate drug metabolism

May alter metabolism of prescribed medications.

Drug & Herb Interactions

High-Risk Interaction Combinations

Bleeding Risk: Dan Shen + Hawthorne Berry

Both herbs have significant blood-thinning properties that create an additive anticoagulant effect, substantially increasing bleeding risk when combined with Tyler's cardiac medications.

Recommendation: Discontinue one of these herbs or implement intensive monitoring protocols.

Sedation Risk: Trazodone + Passionflower

The combination may result in excessive sedation, particularly before veterinary visits.

Recommendation: Monitor for excessive sedation and adjust timing or dosages as needed.

Liver Metabolism: Milk Thistle + Multiple Medications

Milk Thistle affects liver drug metabolism, potentially altering how Tyler's medications are processed.

Recommendation: Regular liver function monitoring with monthly tests initially.

Treatment Optimization

Priority Recommendations

Bleeding Risk Management

Discontinue either Dan Shen or Hawthorne Berry due to their significant blood-thinning properties. Priority for discontinuation should be Hawthorne Berry, as its cardiovascular benefits overlap with Tyler's prescribed cardiac medications.

Monitoring Protocol: Coagulation profiles (PT, PTT, platelet count) every 2 weeks for the first month, then monthly.
Cardiovascular Support Optimization

Separate administration of cardiac medications (furosemide, pimobendan) from herbal supplements by at least 2-3 hours. Administer cardiac medications in the morning and evening, with herbal supplements at midday if continued.

Monitoring Protocol: Blood pressure measurements at every veterinary visit and weekly weight monitoring at home.
Liver Health Monitoring

Establish baseline liver function with comprehensive metabolic panel including ALT, AST, ALP, and bilirubin. Regular monitoring schedule with liver enzymes every 3 months initially.

Monitoring Protocol: Liver enzymes every 3 months initially, extended interval to every 6 months if values remain stable.
Sedation Assessment

Monitor for excessive sedation from the combination of trazodone and Passionflower. Administer Passionflower at least 2 hours apart from trazodone.

Monitoring Protocol: Daily assessment of alertness and responsiveness, with monthly veterinary evaluations.

Emergency Protocols

Immediate Veterinary Contact Required
  • Signs of active bleeding (bloody stools, urine, or vomit)
  • Severe lethargy or collapse
  • Difficulty breathing
  • Seizures or disorientation
  • Jaundice (yellowing of gums or eyes)
Urgent Veterinary Contact (within 24 hours)
  • Excessive bruising
  • Persistent vomiting
  • Loss of appetite for more than 24 hours
  • Significant changes in behavior
  • Inability to urinate
